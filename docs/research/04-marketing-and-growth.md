# Marketing and Growth Plan

Research date: 4 August 2026.

## 1. Marketing thesis

Do not market another social scheduler. Market a safe, multilingual publishing system that takes work from idea to verified result, whether the operator is a person or an agent.

The category promise:

> Turn one sourced idea into platform-native content, approve it once, publish it reliably, and learn what to improve next.

The emotional promise is control. The economic promise is fewer tools, less rework, faster publishing, and better learning. The technical promise is an API/MCP/CLI surface with human governance.

## 2. Positioning

### Primary position

The multilingual publishing control plane for people and agents.

### Proof pillars

1. **Publish with confidence:** true previews, policy checks, approvals, receipts, and connector health.
2. **Adapt, do not duplicate:** platform-native variants and 30-language transcreation with brand glossaries.
3. **Close the loop:** explain what happened, what is uncertain, and what to test next.
4. **Work from anywhere:** web app, Claude, Codex, Hermes, Buzz, MCP, CLI, API, and workflow tools share one safe backend.
5. **Fair economics:** better channel allowances than Postiz, transparent X/AI-media usage, no ambiguous unlimited promise.

### What not to say

- "Go viral with one click."
- "Set and forget autonomous social media."
- "Guaranteed engagement."
- "Post anywhere with no limits."
- "AI replaces your social team."
- "30 languages" before the public capability statement distinguishes UI from content support.
- "Official integration" until the relevant provider approves it.

## 3. Initial ideal customer profiles

### A. Agent-native technical creator

- Solo founder, developer advocate, AI educator, or product builder.
- Uses Codex/Claude Code/Hermes, Markdown, GitHub, n8n, and APIs.
- Needs reliable X, LinkedIn, YouTube/TikTok/Instagram publishing without leaving the workflow.
- Buys the single plan, creates community content, and pulls other technical users.

### B. Multilingual creator or lean brand

- Publishes across two or more languages/markets.
- Current pain is literal translation, inconsistent voice, repetitive cross-posts, and scattered analytics.
- Buys the single plan and values brand glossary, locale approval, per-market links, and feedback.

### C. Small content/automation agency

- 3-15 people, 10-60 social accounts, many approvals, client reporting.
- Needs predictable pricing, separation of client workspaces, roles, shareable approval links, receipts, and bulk operations with guardrails.
- Buys the single plan; strong retention if the workflow is embedded in delivery.

### D. SaaS/workflow builder, after V1

- Wants social publishing inside its product without becoming a platform API expert.
- Starts on the single plan. A separate embedded infrastructure contract can follow when this product surface exists.
- Highest switching cost and potentially lowest churn.

Do not start with large enterprise social teams. Their listening, inbox, compliance archiving, procurement, SSO/SCIM, and support requirements can consume the roadmap before product-market fit.

## 4. One plan, every feature

Postiz currently starts at $29 for five channels and charges more for higher channel allowances. Our public pricing is deliberately simpler:

| Billing | Price | Effective monthly price | Features |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Monthly | $29/month | $29 | every shipped feature |
| Annual | $300/year | $25 | every shipped feature |

The annual option saves $48 versus paying monthly for a year, a 13.8% discount. Market it as `Save $48/year` or `$25/month billed annually`; never show Postiz's `20% off` label because it is not true for our prices. There is no permanently restricted free plan and no Creator/Team/Agency feature gate. Use a seven-day full-product trial with payment method collected through Polar at checkout, `$0 due today`, the exact conversion date/amount, automatic conversion unless canceled, Polar's reminder, and self-service cancellation. A limited sandbox can remain available for developers to test API/MCP schemas without live publishing.

The trial is not a hidden discount: both monthly and annual trials unlock the same product. Measure activation quality, conversion, refund/chargeback rate and retained publishing—not merely how many cards were collected. Enable Polar's repeat-trial abuse prevention, but offer a support path for legitimate edge cases.

### Included in the single plan

- Up to 30 active social channels, with the channel defined as one social account, Page, profile, group, or publication connection.
- Unlimited team members, workspaces/brand groups, drafts, standard scheduled posts, and stored publication receipts under a published fair-use policy.
- Full composer, non-generative picture editor, media library, cross-posting, comments/threads, delays, repeats, RSS automation, sets, signatures, calendar/list views, analytics, status tracking, webhooks, public API, MCP, CLI, and workflow integrations.
- DeepSeek text assistance, content feedback, translation/transcreation, and automation planning.
- Basic Growth Advisor, four-week strategy/cadence, UGC brief, curated promotion opportunities, Creative Tool Radar and Markdown/JSON/YAML export.
- No AI image generation and no AI video generation in V1.

X managed API usage is charged separately at transparent pass-through cost or from a prepaid usage balance because X charges per API operation and charges materially more for URL posts. Other exceptional provider fees may be passed through only when disclosed before the action. There is no media-generation credit system in V1 because the app is not generating media.

### Price validation

Before launch, conduct:

- 15 creator interviews, 10 multilingual brand interviews, 10 agency interviews, 5 SaaS/embedded interviews.
- Van Westendorp price-sensitivity prompts plus forced plan choice using an actual checkout/deposit.
- Landing-page price test by audience, not hidden individual discrimination.
- Track support minutes, provider cost, text-AI cost, Polar fee, storage/egress, and refund by cohort.

Hold gross margin above 75% excluding explicitly passed-through X/provider usage. If support or connector cost makes the plan unsustainable, adjust the clearly disclosed active-channel/fair-use boundary rather than creating hidden feature tiers.

## 5. Growth lessons from Postiz

What worked for Postiz and should be adapted:

- Open source as credibility and distribution.
- Enter underserved provider/ecosystem niches.
- Become the final publish layer in n8n and agent workflows.
- Publish strong docs, examples, and GitHub assets.
- Launch quickly into MCP, skills, CLI, and emerging agent ecosystems.
- Partner with workflow educators whose tutorials sell an outcome.
- Use founder-led long-form content, case studies, and transparent revenue/product lessons.
- Build free tools and track which content drives activation/revenue.
- Progress from tool messaging to result messaging.
- Build an embedded product because infrastructure has lower churn than a dashboard habit.

What to improve:

- Human-edited original content instead of high-volume AI SEO.
- Consent-based partner research instead of scraping and spam-domain tactics.
- Connector reliability and retention before chasing every trend.
- No engagement manipulation features.
- Transparent comparisons and benchmark methodology.
- A content-to-experiment feedback loop that creates recurring value even when users publish less.

## 6. Product-led growth loops

### Loop 1: workflow template

User installs a Codex/Claude/Hermes skill or n8n template, publishes through the product, and shares the workflow. The public template contains an attribution link and an optional remix page. More users import the template, creating new workspaces.

### Loop 2: multilingual result

A creator adapts a successful source into several markets, uses a shareable report to explain differences, and publishes a case study. The case study links to a reusable brand glossary/content workflow.

### Loop 3: approval and report

Agency sends a no-login approval/report link to a client. The client experiences the product and later creates another workspace or refers another team.

### Loop 4: open developer ecosystem

Open-source connector SDK, CLI, schema examples, MCP definitions, and test harness gain GitHub discovery and contributions. The hosted control plane remains the easiest secure deployment.

### Loop 5: evidence-based free tool

A useful calculator/checker produces a saved brief or draft that continues into the product. Examples: X URL-post cost estimator, social media size validator, multilingual hook comparison, posting policy preflight, or experiment planner. Do not build thin keyword pages with no standalone value.

## 6A. Customer-facing Growth Advisor

V1 offers a basic advisor that helps customers answer four practical questions:

1. Who are we trying to reach, on which channels, and why?
2. What recurring content can we credibly publish with our current capacity?
3. Where could this product be launched, listed, discussed or contributed without spamming?
4. Which specialist tools are currently worth evaluating for the required creative workflow?

The output is a four-week plan, not a generic 50-page strategy. It contains three to five content pillars, channel/format choices, sustainable cadence, weekly series, CTA library, localization notes, UGC concept, experiment tags and measurement. Users can view Markdown or copy JSON/YAML for Codex, Claude, Hermes, an internal repository or workflow automation.

### Promotion and backlink opportunities

The AI does not search the open web and invent directory links. It ranks a curated, versioned opportunity catalog that the owner will populate later with official URLs and reviewed rules. Candidate types include:

- Product-launch and startup directories.
- Relevant software/review directories.
- Integration and automation marketplaces.
- Community resource/showcase threads that explicitly permit submissions.
- Partner ecosystems and integration directories.
- Guest tutorials, case studies, podcasts, newsletters and expert roundups.
- Open-source `awesome` lists or documentation resources where contribution rules permit the project.

Treat backlinks as a possible by-product of useful, relevant placement—not a quantity target. Show fit, audience, requirements, effort, cost, disclosure, last-verified date and a suggested asset/pitch. Never auto-submit forms, scrape contacts, mass-email, pay for undisclosed links or guarantee ranking/reach. The user owns the final submission.

### Creative Tool Radar

Maintain an editorial catalog for video, image, audio, avatar/UGC, clipping, design, research and workflow tools. Every record needs an official URL, use case, limitations, pricing model/date checked, rights/privacy caveat, supported integrations, change log and affiliate status. Recommendations are contextual and capped; never dump a long sponsored directory.

- Review high-impact records weekly and the full active catalog monthly.
- Publish only material tool changes and update affected workflow guides.
- Show `last verified` and `may have changed` instead of pretending permanent accuracy.
- Keep recommendation ranking independent of affiliate commission; disclose every commercial relationship.
- Let users follow a workflow category and opt into a monthly digest or material-change alerts.

## 7. Content engine

### Editorial standard

Every substantial article must have at least two of:

- Original product data with an explained sample and privacy threshold.
- Reproducible workflow/template.
- Screenshots or a video created from a real account/demo.
- Primary-source platform citations and a last-verified date.
- Named practitioner interview.
- Before/after example reviewed by a native speaker.
- Clear limitations and what did not work.

AI can research, outline, transcreate, check, and format. A named human owns the claim, edits the piece, and updates it. Do not publish dozens of unreviewed synthetic articles.

### Blog and resource architecture

1. `/guides/platforms/`: current API/review/policy guides.
2. `/workflows/`: Codex, Claude, Hermes, Buzz, n8n, creative tool, and campaign recipes.
3. `/languages/`: transcreation guides and locale-specific examples.
4. `/benchmarks/`: transparent aggregated benchmarks and experiments.
5. `/stories/`: customer results with actual methodology and permission.
6. `/compare/`: honest alternatives/comparisons.
7. `/tools/`: free validators/calculators/templates.
8. `/tool-radar/`: dated specialist-tool profiles, comparisons and workflow handoffs.
9. `/opportunities/`: curated launch/listing/community categories and submission guidance without bulk automation.
10. `/changelog/`, `/status/`, `/docs/`, `/methodology/`.

Use author profiles, reviewer names, verified dates, sources, change history, and structured data. A proper CMS workflow includes draft, technical review, editorial review, legal/platform review where required, localization, publish, update reminder, and retirement.

### UGC and creator program

- Recruit 20 small, credible educators across agent workflows, localization, creator video, and agency operations.
- Pay for work, not praise. A tutorial contract requires disclosure and does not require a positive conclusion.
- Give a sandbox/demo workspace, verified examples, technical support, and tracking link.
- Offer 20-30% recurring affiliate commission for up to 12 months, subject to margin and fraud review.
- Monthly workflow bounties for reproducible templates, with rights/licensing stated.
- Customer story consent is separate from product access and revocable for future use.
- Never buy reviews, votes, ratings, installs, or undisclosed endorsements.

The customer-facing V1 UGC advisor should stay deliberately basic: propose one campaign goal, creator/customer profile, five prompt angles, a short brief template, rights/consent and disclosure checklist, approval criteria, distribution plan and measurement. It does not discover or contact creators automatically, negotiate contracts, synthesize testimonials or generate avatar/video assets.

## 8. Distribution sites and channels

Prioritize relevance over listing count. Verify each community's self-promotion rules before posting.

### Product/developer launches

- Product Hunt: one meaningful launch per major product story, with real maker participation and no vote manipulation.
- Show HN: technical launch when the API/MCP/connector SDK is demonstrable and the team can answer implementation questions.
- GitHub: open SDK/CLI/examples, issue templates, roadmap, docs, and releases.
- Indie Hackers: transparent build/revenue/product lessons.
- DEV Community and Hashnode: technical tutorials with canonical strategy to avoid duplicate SEO confusion.
- HackerNoon/Medium: selective syndication after first-party publication.

### Communities

- Reddit communities such as self-hosted, SaaS, n8n, social media marketing, and language/creator niches only when the post independently answers a real question and complies with subreddit rules.
- Lemmy self-hosting/open-source communities if an actual self-hostable component exists.
- n8n, Make, Zapier, Pipedream, Codex, Claude, Hermes, Buzz, and creator-tool communities through official integrations/templates.
- Small founder communities and local multilingual marketing communities through participation, demos, and office hours.

### Directories/review sites

- AlternativeTo and SaaSHub for accurate alternative/category discovery.
- BetaList/Uneed during beta if current submission terms fit.
- G2 and Capterra after enough real customers can review without incentives tied to sentiment.
- AI directories such as Futurepedia or There's An AI For That only if they still provide qualified traffic; measure activation, not backlinks alone.
- MCP/agent registries and official skill marketplaces when the surface is stable and approved.
- Automation marketplaces: n8n community/official node, Zapier integration, Make app, Pipedream components.

Avoid irrelevant paid directories, false "open source" listings, mass guest-post networks, and copied comparison roundups. Maintain a distribution ledger with audience, cost, rules, URL, visits, signups, activated workspaces, paid conversions, and assisted revenue.

## 9. Comparison strategy

Create comparison pages for Postiz, Buffer, Hootsuite, Later, Metricool, Publer, SocialBee, Typefully, and developer-focused publishing APIs only after current fact checks.

Every page must:

- State who each product is best for.
- Date the research and link primary pricing/capability sources.
- Compare account count, post limits, team/approval, API/MCP, languages, analytics, video, embedded, self-hosting, support, and estimated external API costs.
- Distinguish "we do not support this" from "provider does not allow this."
- Avoid trademark confusion, fake endorsements, and unprovable superlatives.
- Offer a migration checklist and export/import tool where lawful.
- Include a correction contact and update schedule.

Do not use competitors' customer logos/testimonials or copy their UI. "Alternative to" content should remain useful even if the reader chooses the competitor.

## 10. Launch sequence

### Days 0-30: evidence and design partners

- Interview 40 prospects across the first three ICPs.
- Publish a live connector-approval journey and policy guide.
- Recruit 25 design partners with a written beta agreement.
- Ship waitlist segmented by ICP and desired platforms/languages.
- Start founder build notes twice weekly, focused on decisions and failures.

- Publish CLI/MCP quickstarts and recorded end-to-end demo.

- Capture activation recordings/interviews with permission.
- Seed 5 high-quality workflow partners, not 50 shallow affiliates.

### Days 61-90: paid beta

- Charge the $29 monthly or $300 annual plan; free feedback alone does not validate willingness to pay.
- Launch on Product Hunt only if onboarding and provider reliability are ready.
- Share a technical Show HN/GitHub story separately if there is real open/developer value.
- Publish three case studies with baseline, workflow, timeframe, actual metrics, and limitations.
- Start a monthly transparent reliability/connector report.

### Days 91-180: compound channels

- Double down on the two channels producing activated paid workspaces, not raw traffic.
- Launch n8n integration and creator workflows.
- Add localized landing/resources based on demand and reviewer capacity.
- Produce one definitive report/benchmark per quarter, not weekly shallow stats.
- Expand to 15-20 partners with performance and quality review.
- Begin Embedded design-partner program with 3-5 SaaS companies.
- Test paid search only for proven high-intent jobs and competitor terms allowed by ad/trademark policy.

## 11. Marketing calendar

Weekly operating cadence:

- Monday: platform/policy update or technical guide.
- Tuesday: founder build lesson and product changelog.
- Wednesday: customer/workflow demo or live office hour.
- Thursday: multilingual before/after or analytics experiment.
- Friday: community answers, template release, and content maintenance.

One excellent item can be transcreated into several native pieces, but do not publish identical text across networks. Each platform version should match audience and format. Set safe cadence defaults and let people opt down.

## 12. Lifecycle marketing

### Activation

- Immediately after checkout: confirm `$0 charged`, selected interval, exact first charge and cancellation path.
- Day 0: get one account connected and one post drafted or scheduled; do not open with a feature tour.
- Day 2: resume unfinished setup or show the user's first receipt/analytics state.
- Day 4: Polar sends the required pre-conversion reminder for this seven-day trial; our product may add a useful progress summary, but must not obscure or contradict the billing reminder.
- Day 6: concise final trial status with exact amount/date, value achieved, export and `Manage or cancel` link.
- Day 7: send either payment receipt and continuity message, cancellation confirmation, or failed-payment remediation—never a misleading success message.
- Welcome message based on ICP with one next action.
- Connection reminder only if permissions/review are clear.
- Draft rescue after an abandoned composer, preserving content.
- First publication receipt and explanation of analytics freshness.

### Retention

- Weekly planned-versus-published summary and action-required accounts.
- Experiment completion notification with one evidence-backed next test.
- Monthly usage/cost/connector-health report.
- Content opportunity reminders based on user-created cadence, not unsolicited AI-generated noise.
- Token expiry/reconnection reminders with direct remediation.

### Cancellation

- Trial cancellation must be possible from Settings without contacting support and must confirm that no charge will be attempted when canceled before conversion.
- Ask reason, allow pause/read-only/archive, and show data export/deletion.
- Offer downgrade only when it fits the stated reason, not a manipulative maze.
- Categorize churn by posting stopped, missing connector/feature, reliability, price, AI quality, support, seasonality, or business closed.
- Feed reasons into roadmap and publish aggregate improvements.

## 13. Metrics

### North-star candidate

Weekly verified publications that satisfy the workspace's approval policy and produce a valid external receipt. This measures real delivered value, not scheduled intentions.

Pair it with retention and quality so volume does not encourage spam.

### Funnel

- Visitor to signup by intent/page.
- Signup to first connected account.
- Connected account to first validated draft.
- Draft to approved scheduled/verified publication.
- Time to first verified publication, target under 10 minutes for a simple text account.
- Second-week verified publication rate.
- 30/90-day workspace and revenue retention.

### Product quality

- Valid publish execution success by connector, target 99.5% under defined exclusions.
- Duplicate publication count, target zero.
- Median remediation time.
- Analytics coverage/freshness.
- Approval turnaround.
- User acceptance/edit rate for AI suggestions by locale.
- Support tickets per 100 active workspaces.

### Business

- MRR, net revenue retention, logo churn, expansion, refund/chargeback.
- Gross margin including Polar, provider APIs, AI, storage/egress, and support.
- CAC/payback by channel and ICP.
- Activated paid workspaces and assisted revenue per content/partner.
- Embedded active customer connections and usage retention.

### Guardrail metrics

- Platform warnings/enforcement and revoked applications.
- Spam/abuse reports per 1,000 publications.
- Bulk/duplicate/cadence blocks.
- User-data deletion time.
- AI disclosure/accessibility completion.
- Partner disclosure compliance and fraudulent referrals.

## 14. Sales motion

- Self-serve single plan for creators, teams, and agencies.
- Optional paid onboarding or migration service, without withholding product features.
- Founder-led design partnerships for future Embedded infrastructure: discovery, security/architecture review, sandbox, usage proposal, and paid pilot.
- Do not promise connectors pending review or features on a vision page as shipped.
- Keep a public security pack, architecture summary, subprocessors, status, DPA request path, and deletion documentation.

## 15. Budget priorities

For the first six months, spend in this order:

1. Provider review/compliance and connector reliability.
2. Product/design quality and onboarding research.
3. Technical/editorial content with real experts and native reviewers.
4. Workflow educators and high-quality UGC.
5. Customer support and success.
6. Select directory/tool sponsorships with attribution.
7. Paid search after intent and retention are proven.

Do not spend heavily on broad influencer posts, vanity launch votes, or undifferentiated AI SEO.

## 16. Marketing team handoff checklist

- Use only approved capability and pricing data from a dated source.
- Every platform claim has an owner and recheck date.
- Every performance claim includes timeframe, sample/method, and customer consent.
- AI-assisted content has a named human editor.
- Affiliate/sponsored content is disclosed clearly.
- Community posts follow local rules and add standalone value.
- Avoid copying Postiz language, screenshots, illustrations, or article structure.
- Do not call integrations official before provider/partner approval.
- Maintain a correction log, content-update schedule, and sunset stale pages.
- Optimize for activated, retained workspaces and verified publications, not impressions alone.
